# What the duplicate search costs

v0.6's pipeline is staged so the expensive stage runs on almost nothing. This is
the measurement of whether that is true, and of what the default floor buys.

## How

```bash
just bench-dup ~/Downloads
DT_DUP_MIN=0         just bench-dup ~/Downloads
DT_DUP_MIN=104857600 just bench-dup ~/Projects
```

`core/src/duplicates.rs::diagnostics::over_a_real_tree`, release build, macOS
15.5 on Apple Silicon, APFS. **Warm cache** except where noted — the figures
below are the pipeline's own cost, not the disk's. The built-in rules are in
force, so `node_modules` and `target/` are pruned, which is the configuration a
first run actually gets.

## The funnel

| Tree | Floor | Eligible | Contested | Hashed | Read | Groups | Reclaimable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `~/Downloads` | 1 MiB | 1,397 files / 34.5 GiB | 539 (39%) | 1,076 | 3.1 GiB | 247 | 1.65 GiB |
| `~/Downloads` | 0 | 12,007 files / 34.5 GiB | 4,101 (34%) | 6,009 | 3.2 GiB | 1,060 | 1.73 GiB |
| `~/Projects` | 1 MiB | 16,850 files / 97.3 GiB | 15,046 (89%) | 30,022 | 52.7 GiB | 1,585 | 46.6 GiB |
| `~/Projects` | 100 MiB | 76 files / 39.5 GiB | 5 (7%) | 10 | 3.7 GiB | 1 | 2.9 GiB |

*Eligible* is what survives the rules and the floor. *Contested* is what shares
its size with something else — the only files any byte of which is read.
*Hashed* counts hashing operations, so a file that reaches the full hash after
the prefix hash appears twice.

**Bucketing by size is what makes this affordable.** On `~/Downloads` 61% of
eligible files have a unique size and are never opened; 9% of the eligible bytes
are read in total. A unique size is proof of unique content, and it comes free
with the scan.

**`~/Projects` is the adversarial case**, and shows what the claim does not
cover: 89% of files there share a size with something, because a project tree is
full of identical small build artefacts, lockfiles and vendored copies. The
funnel narrows almost nothing, and the search reads half the tree.

## Time

| Tree | Floor | Scan | Search | Read throughput |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `~/Downloads` | 1 MiB | 186 ms | 943 ms | 3.3 GiB/s (warm) |
| `~/Projects` | 1 MiB | 14.2 s | 12.5 s | 4.2 GiB/s (warm) |
| `~/Projects` | 100 MiB | 15.9 s | 4.4 s | **850 MiB/s** |

The last row is the honest one. Its 76 eligible files are large enough not to be
in the page cache, so 850 MiB/s is what the disk gives — and it is the figure to
plan by, since the tree a user runs this on for the first time is cold.

**blake3 is not the bound.** The `pure` feature drops the hand-written assembly
(see `core/Cargo.toml` for why: `just lint-windows` cannot assemble on a Unix
host), and the warm rows still move 3–4 GiB/s through the hasher on one machine's
worth of rayon threads. The disk gives a fifth of that.

## What the floor is worth

Dropping the floor to 0 on `~/Downloads` costs 5.6× the hashing operations and
finds **4.3× the groups for 5% more reclaimable space**. That is the trade the
1 MiB default exists to refuse: 813 further groups, none of them worth reading a
report about, and a report 800 lines longer.

Raising it to 100 MiB on `~/Projects` turns a 12.5-second search into a
4.4-second one and still finds 2.9 GiB — 6% of what the full run finds, but in a
single group a user can act on immediately.

## What is not measured here

- **Cold-cache whole-tree runs.** Purging the page cache needs `sudo` on macOS,
  so only the incidentally-cold row above stands for it.
- **Windows and Linux.** The pipeline is `std::fs` and rayon throughout with no
  platform split of its own; the one platform-specific fact it depends on —
  whether `created()` is recorded — is a correctness question, answered by
  `keeper_fell_back`, not a performance one.
- **The plan and the report.** Both are linear in the number of copies found, in
  the hundreds, and disappear beside a single read.
