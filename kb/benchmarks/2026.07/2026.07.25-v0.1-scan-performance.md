# Benchmark: disk-tools v0.1 scan performance and memory

Measurements for [Task 11](../../specs/2026.07/2026.07.14-disk-tools-v0.1-scan-report.md)
of the v0.1 spec: prove the "fast" claim, and quantify the cost of keeping the whole
tree in memory.

## Environment

| | |
|---|---|
| Machine | Mac16,5 — 16 cores, 64 GB RAM |
| OS | macOS 26.5.2 |
| Filesystem | APFS (internal SSD, `/dev/disk3s1s1`) |
| Binary | `cargo build --release` |
| `hyperfine` | 1.20.0 |
| `diskus` | 0.9.0 |
| `du` | BSD `du` shipped with macOS (`/usr/bin/du`, no `--version`) |

`diskus` and `hyperfine` were installed with `cargo install`, not Homebrew —
`brew install` failed on an unwritable `/opt/homebrew/etc` and would have needed
`sudo`.

**The machine was not quiet.** Two real-time antivirus products (Bitdefender,
Kaspersky) index every file written, and the 28 GB of fixtures triggered a scan that
held the load average above 13 for more than ten minutes. All numbers below come from
a later, calmer window (combined AV CPU ~85%, load ~20), and `min` is reported
alongside `mean ± σ` because it is the statistic least contaminated by such spikes.
See [Caveats](#caveats).

## Method

Fixtures are generated by [`scripts/bench-fixtures.sh`](../../../scripts/bench-fixtures.sh)
(`just bench-fixtures <dir>`), which seeds its RNG with a fixed value so two runs
produce identical trees. Files carry real bytes; nothing is `truncate`d into a sparse
file, which would collapse `allocated` to ~0 and make the reported sizes meaningless.

| Fixture | Shape | Entries | On disk |
|---------|-------|---------|---------|
| `node_modules/` | 300 packages × 350 files of 1–8 KiB, each package nested 1–5 levels deep | 105,000 files / 1,201 dirs | 645 MB |
| `cache/` | 200 dirs × 100 files of 4 KiB–2 MiB, each nested 1–3 levels deep | 20,000 files / 602 dirs | 20 GB |
| `media/` | 40 flat files of 200 MiB | 40 files / 1 dir | 7.8 GB |

Timings come from `just bench <dir>`, which runs exactly:

```bash
hyperfine -N --warmup 5 --runs 20 --output=null \
  -n "disk-tools --depth 0"   "<bin> <fixture> --depth 0" \
  -n "disk-tools (full tree)" "<bin> <fixture>" \
  -n "du -sh"                 "du -sh <fixture>" \
  -n "diskus"                 "diskus <fixture>"
```

- `-N` (no intermediate shell). With a shell, the ~2–3 ms spawn cost dominated the
  `media` fixture and inflated `du`'s variance.
- `--warmup 5` satisfies the spec's **warm cache** condition; `--output=null`
  discards stdout, so rendering is compared on equal terms.
- **`--depth 0` is the like-for-like comparison with `du -sh`**: both then print a
  single summary line. The default invocation additionally formats one line per
  entry — 105,000 of them for `node_modules` — which `du -sh` never does. Both are
  reported, because they answer different questions.

## Results — wall clock

20 runs, warm cache. Bold marks the `--depth 0` column that AC2 turns on.

| Fixture | `disk-tools --depth 0` | `disk-tools` (full tree) | `du -sh` | `diskus` |
|---------|------------------------|--------------------------|----------|----------|
| `node_modules` | **167.0 ms ± 11.8** (min 146.5) | 220.9 ms ± 12.3 (min 201.0) | 344.6 ms ± 48.7 (min 300.0) | 100.4 ms ± 8.9 (min 87.1) |
| `cache` | **32.3 ms ± 1.6** (min 29.9) | 43.8 ms ± 1.5 (min 41.7) | 59.6 ms ± 2.1 (min 54.7) | 24.4 ms ± 1.4 (min 22.7) |
| `media` | **3.5 ms ± 0.3** (min 3.1) | 3.5 ms ± 0.3 (min 3.1) | 6.7 ms ± 0.5 (min 5.8) | 4.7 ms ± 0.3 (min 4.3) |

Speed-up over `du -sh`, from the means (higher is better):

| Fixture | `disk-tools --depth 0` | `disk-tools` (full tree) | `diskus` |
|---------|------------------------|--------------------------|----------|
| `node_modules` | **2.06×** | 1.56× | 3.43× |
| `cache` | **1.84×** | 1.36× | 2.44× |
| `media` | **1.91×** | 1.91× | 1.43× |

An independent earlier series over the same fixtures agreed on every ordering; the
`min` values matched within ~10% for `disk-tools` and `diskus`, and within 30% for
`du`, which was consistently the noisiest command.

### Reading

- **`disk-tools` beats `du -sh` on every fixture, in both invocations** — 1.8–2.1×
  with matched output, 1.4–1.9× even while rendering the entire tree that `du -sh`
  never prints. The spec's warm-cache criterion is met without needing the
  `--depth 0` caveat.
- **The gap widens with directory count.** Single-threaded `du` degrades as the tree
  gets deeper and wider; the parallel scanners absorb it. On an earlier, flatter
  version of the `node_modules` fixture (901 dirs instead of 1,201) `du -sh` came in
  at 166.8 ms and was *faster* than a full-tree `disk-tools` run. The fixture was
  regenerated after a review found an off-by-one that left a fifth of the packages
  completely un-nested; these are the corrected numbers.
- **Rendering the full tree costs real time**: +54 ms for 105,000 lines, +12 ms for
  20,000, +0.0 ms for 41. That is the price of the report over a bare total.
- **`diskus` is 1.3–1.7× faster than `disk-tools --depth 0`** on the metadata-heavy
  fixtures, and this is expected: it accumulates a single total and keeps nothing.
  `disk-tools` allocates a `PathBuf` and a `ScanNode` per entry and builds the tree
  that directory totals and (v0.4) the TUI need. On `media`, where entry count stops
  mattering, `disk-tools` is the fastest of the three.
- **System time exceeds wall time** for both parallel scanners — on `node_modules`,
  `disk-tools` spent ~1,050 ms of system time inside a ~220 ms run, against `du`'s
  ~300 ms inside ~345 ms. That is 16 cores issuing `stat` calls concurrently, and it
  is what buys the wall-clock win over single-threaded `du`.

## Results — peak memory

`/usr/bin/time -l` (`just bench-memory <path> [ARGS]`), warm cache, on two **real**
trees. Entry counts are `find <dir> | wc -l`; neither scan reported a skipped entry.
GB/MB are decimal (10⁹ / 10⁶ bytes), matching what `time -l` reports in raw bytes.

| Tree | Entries | Peak RSS | Bytes/entry |
|------|---------|----------|-------------|
| `~/Projects`, `--depth 0` | 2,247,326 | 1,424,179,200 B ≈ 1.42 GB | 634 B |
| `~/Projects`, full tree | 2,247,326 | 1,333,379,072 B ≈ 1.33 GB | 593 B |
| `~/Library`, `--depth 0` | 1,400,409 | 867,647,488 B ≈ 868 MB | 620 B |

### Reading

- **≈ 600–640 bytes per entry**, consistent across two independent trees of very
  different shape. The spec's ≥1M-file criterion is met twice over.
- The full-tree render measured *lower* than `--depth 0` on the same tree — a ~7%
  run-to-run swing in allocator behaviour, not a real reduction. Read the two as the
  same number: the rendered `String` is not a meaningful share of peak.
- Extrapolating: a 10M-entry scan would want roughly **6 GB**. That is the price of
  the design decision in §4 ("full tree retained in memory — accepted trade-off"),
  and the number to revisit if a streaming mode is ever considered.
- Wall-clock for these scans (13–16 s) is *not* comparable to the fixture timings —
  they were taken during the antivirus storm described above. Only the RSS figures
  are used here, and peak RSS does not depend on background CPU load.

## Caveats

- **Background load.** Two real-time antivirus daemons and Spotlight are always
  running on this machine; combined AV CPU was ~85% even in the calmest window
  available. A dedicated quiet machine would produce lower absolute numbers for all
  four commands. The *ordering* was stable across two independent series, which is
  what the conclusions rest on.
- `du -sh` is the least stable of the four (σ up to 48 ms, with outliers past 1 s
  under load). Its `min` is the more trustworthy figure and is reported alongside.
- Single machine, single filesystem. APFS on an internal SSD is the best case for a
  metadata-bound walk; network shares and spinning disks were not measured.
- `du -sh` and `diskus` produce a **total**; `disk-tools --depth 0` produces a total
  plus a bar. The three do comparable work, not identical work.
- APFS COW clones are overcounted by `disk-tools` (a documented v0.1 limitation), so
  the *sizes* it reports for the real trees may exceed what deleting them would
  reclaim. This affects neither the timings nor the memory figures.
- Only aggregate numbers from the real trees are recorded here — no path listings.
- The `cache` fixture came out at 20 GB rather than the ~2 GB first sketched: a
  uniform 4 KiB–2 MiB draw averages ~1 MiB, not ~100 KiB. The shape is still the
  intended middle ground and the numbers are reported as measured.

## Reproducing

```bash
just bench-fixtures /tmp/dt-bench    # ~28 GB, a few minutes
just bench          /tmp/dt-bench
just bench-memory   ~/Projects --depth 0
rm -rf /tmp/dt-bench
```

Let the machine settle after generating the fixtures — writing 28 GB wakes every
indexer and antivirus scanner on the system, and timing through that measures them,
not the scanner.
